## One Turn per Channel

An agent executes one turn at a time in a given channel. While a turn is live, that channel is closed to further turns for that agent. Live means calling the model, running a tool, or blocked on an approval. Other channels are unaffected. The channel is the unit of concurrency, and also the unit of context and of intervention. What is happening in a channel is one thing, and stopping a channel stops it.

Only one approval prompt can be live for a given agent in a given channel.

## The Queue

A message addressed to a busy agent is queued, never dropped. The framework acknowledges it with a 👀 reaction rather than a reply, to avoid adding posts to the channel where approval prompts live.

The queue drains rather than pops. When a turn ends in a way that allows progress and the channel goes idle, one new turn consumes everything queued there. Ten messages sent during a long turn become one turn, not ten. The queue itself holds pointers rather than content: the fact of unprocessed work and where it starts. The content arrives through the channel history. When the assembled context can't reach back as far as the earliest unprocessed post, the new turn's status post says how far back it saw.

The backlog is unbounded and nothing expires. If approvals are slow, work accumulates behind them. The alternative is discarding work silently; an unbounded queue at least fails visibly.

## How Turns End

Every way a turn can end has a defined, visible outcome. Normal completion posts its answer. A denial ends the turn with the agent asking how to proceed. Budget exhaustion asks to extend. A malformed tool call ends immediately with the error posted. A mutating call that timed out ends with an explicit statement that completion can't be confirmed. A model-provider outage ends with the failure posted. Human intervention posts what it did: `/stop` at the next boundary, `/kill` immediately. In every case, the channel is released and the record says what happened.

The framework never adapts quietly. Transport errors such as timeouts and rate limits are retried invisibly because they carry no intent. Everything else stops. Feeding errors back for the model to retry is the mechanism behind improvisation. A model told a tool doesn't exist will guess name variants, and if one lands, it has succeeded at a call nobody intended. For an agent whose ambiguous call was an email, a retry duplicates the email.

## Restart

On restart, whether deploy or crash, all in-flight turns are abandoned and every agent boots idle. Resuming would mean re-running tool calls that are not idempotent, or executing intentions formed before the deploy. Instead, the framework invalidates pending approval prompts so a stale prompt can't be clicked into action. It backfills channel history. The system bot posts one notice naming the downtime window and stating that in-flight work was abandoned. Queued work and outstanding triggers survive and drain once channels go idle. What is lost is one turn's progress per channel.

## Loop Control

Agent-to-agent mentions make chains possible. The framework bounds them in code.

Depth: every turn carries a depth counter the model never sees. Human-initiated work starts at zero. Trigger-initiated work starts at one. Each agent-to-agent handoff adds one. At the limit, further handoffs are refused, and the turn posts that a person needs to pick the work up.

Width: one, by the mention rule. Each turn can hand work to at most one colleague, so depth bounds total work.

Framework-wide: a ceiling on turns started per rolling hour. A breach halts every agent, posts a notice, and stays until a human posts `/resume`. The halt exists to stop a runaway loop and put a person in front of it.
